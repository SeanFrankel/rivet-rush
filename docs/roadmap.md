## Product contract

The owner's target is a movement-driven 2D MOBA for platform-fighter and MOBA players, with excellent mechanics, a fresh take on the genre, easy pickup sessions with friends and skill improvement through ranked play. Main mode: **4v4**. Average match duration: **15–20 minutes**. Earlier guidance allowed 30-minute-plus games; the latest preference treats very long games as exhausting and generally a failure to close. No exact maximum or termination rule is decided. Intended arc: minutes **1–5 laning**, **6–15 midgame teamfighting and heavy farming**, and **15+ endgame**. These are intended phases, not authorization to implement rigid timed restrictions. 1v1 and 2v2 are optional practice/alternative modes for beta.

Provisional progression: laning earns two or three early items (or one major item) and levels that unlock the ultimate; midgame expands rotations, farming, teamfights and objective-taking; endgame reaches full levels and roughly six items before a decisive objective/teamfight. Death downtime is provisionally about ten seconds early to one minute late, influenced by game stage and farm, with the formula and travel-time interpretation unresolved.

Six heroes, four active abilities per hero, a melee/ranged mix and three maps remain established scope. Aim precision should not dominate combat. Some crowd control is wanted, but loss of character control should not feel longer than roughly one or two seconds; exact rules remain provisional. Farming should confer a moderate advantage, last hitting must exist in some form, and secondary objectives should allow teams to recover or extend advantages through play. Deadlock-like last hitting is an inspiration to discuss, not an approved mechanical specification.

Ranked mode is now part of the intended reason to return. Its release timing, matchmaking, rating rules and account requirements remain undecided under D7; do not interpret this as either an approved beta feature or a feature to defer indefinitely. D5 establishes two lanes as the standard, varied maps, an ideal support/carry/brawler/jungler composition without mandatory role locks, and minimap/vision/ward systems. Their detailed rules remain to be specified. Platform, controller support, first-release scope, art direction and playtest arrangements remain open. The old desktop-browser/private-beta recommendations are proposals, not accepted owner decisions.

**Planning impact, not implementation authorization:** four pilots per crew and a longer match arc require revisiting lane structure, map occupancy, roster/composition rules, farming and upgrade pacing, respawn/travel costs, objective schedules, siege/endgame rules, eight-seat rooms, replay/protocol layouts and eight-human validation. The current five-minute meltdown and seven-minute draw are prototype behavior incompatible with the new target. Do not merely raise the timer or seat count and declare the design fulfilled. Rebaseline milestones after the remaining decisions and the owner's instruction to resume.

#### D3 · What should win a fight, and what must the defender be able to do?

**Owner answer · 2026-11-03 · Provisional guidance; more thought requested:** Aiming should not be too important. Some CC is desirable, but it must not feel overpowering or leave the player unable to control their character for longer than roughly **one or two seconds at most**. The owner explicitly regards this as initial guidance, not a complete combat contract. **Still to resolve:** the intended relative value of movement, combos and team setup; targeting/aim assistance; whether the control-loss limit applies to the entire combined CC chain; and how soft slows, launches and forced movement count. Do not convert the approximate guidance into an invented exact stun cap or choose a CC-protection mechanic without discussion. **Affected work:** targeting, ability interactions, counterplay and input methods.

**Owner choice:** set the priority among aim precision, movement/spacing, ability combinations and team setup. Specify whether long committed combos and extended loss of control fit the intended game. Confirm the accessibility role of assisted keyboard attacks relative to mouse aim.

**Recommendation:** movement, spacing and coordinated setup lead; aiming and combos reward mastery within that framework. Preserve useful keyboard-assisted attacks, but do not assume their parity with mouse aim is proven. Strong displacement or burst should offer an understandable answer through a tell, range restriction, commitment or recovery. Avoid extended chains that deny the defender a meaningful opportunity to act. Melee should have viable approaches and ranged should have viable spacing answers, rather than either archetype winning by default.

**Why this matters:** an impressive sequence for its user can be an uninteresting death for its target. The designer sets acceptable agency and commitment; agents tune exact damage, cooldowns, cancel windows and assistance within that contract. Do not impose a universal time-to-kill number before observing real pairings and wave interference.

**Evidence / timing:** resolve the contract before deeper combat tuning (W1/W5). In swapped melee/ranged matchups and both input methods, record successful approaches, successful escapes, unavoidable-looking chains and whether a losing player can identify and execute a response. Proposed numerical limits should emerge from those observations, not another game's values.

#### D4 · How much power should farming buy, and how can a losing crew recover?

**Owner answer · 2026-11-03 · Principles approved; mechanics partly open:** A farm lead should primarily provide a **moderate advantage**. **Last hitting must be present on some level**, potentially inspired by Deadlock's version. Secondary objectives should support comebacks or increased leads according to how teams play and use their advantages. **Still to resolve:** how a 2D last hit is secured/contested, whether denial exists, individual versus nearby/team income, and how objectives reward a trailing team without erasing an earned lead. Deadlock is a reference, not authorization to copy an unspecified current rule. **Affected work:** laning incentives, team roles, income distribution, upgrade pacing and objective rewards. Numerical tuning follows an agreed model and playtests.

**Owner choice:** should a farm lead primarily provide a modest advantage in otherwise contestable fights, or create a large power gap that requires avoiding combat? Should last hitting be a demanding individual resource contest or an execution bonus on top of shared team income?

**Recommendation:** keep team income with meaningful last-hit/pickup bonuses and a small match-local shop. A farm lead should help win the next decision without routinely deciding the entire match. Preserve ways to recover through a punished overcommitment, a contested pickup/objective, or a well-defended push. Avoid automatic equalization that erases successful play. Defer permanent combat-power unlocks.

**Why this matters:** too little reward disconnects farming from the MOBA loop; too much makes a movement-focused fight irrelevant after an early mistake. Easy recovery can also erase the satisfaction of earning a lead. This is an intended advantage curve, not a request for the owner to set every item price.

**Evidence / timing:** decide before expanding the shop/progression (W5). Compare fights at different earned upgrade leads, record whether disadvantaged crews have credible choices, and inspect both comebacks and games where the leader closes cleanly. Agent-owned experiments may tune payouts and upgrade values; a shift to a large item catalog or individual-only economy requires a recorded scope decision.

## Engineering contract for the online match

- **Authority and time.** The server steps at a target 60 Hz and starts at 20 snapshots/s. Only the server resolves damage, cooldowns, currency, win state and seat ownership. Inputs carry match/seat identity, sequence and held/pressed actions. Deduplicate edge actions, discard old-match inputs and bound accepted message rate. Interpolate other entities. Predict local movement and reconcile to the acknowledged input. Test jumps, bridge expiry and buys under delayed or duplicated input.
- **Lifecycle.** Drive lobby → ready → playing → result → lobby. A rematch gets a new match ID and fresh simulation. An online tab losing focus releases held keys while the server continues. Give a disconnected seat an idle-safe/bot takeover, reserve it for 60 seconds, and reclaim it with a separate opaque reconnect token. Expired reservations stay bots until match end. Ship team identification via labels or silhouettes as well as color. Bind controls and accessibility guidance to the real HUD.

- **Diagnostics.** For each room capture anonymous match ID, revision, server region, join/start/end/leave reason, tick latency, late-input counts and structured fault code. ## Release night and evidence

1. **Before the first link.** Host attaches commit/tag, artifact hashes, known issues, supported clients, matrix results, human session notes and last-good tag to the release record. Record open issues by impact. Any issue exposing tokens, affecting another room, allowing a forged result, repeatedly crashing a match or blocking a supported join/reconnect is release-blocking. Give every lower-impact issue an owner and workaround.
2. **During the canary.** Watch completed/started matches, join failures, disconnect reasons, p95 tick and memory against the same staging budgets. One wrong winner, token leak or cross-room action immediately stops invites. Two join failures among the first eight valid attempts, or two server-aborted matches in any ten, pause expansion for diagnosis. If root cause remains unknown after ten minutes, drain and roll back.
3. **Restore.** Stop admitting rooms, allow healthy rooms to finish with a bounded drain, then switch client and server to the last-good pair. For an integrity or exposure incident, stop affected rooms immediately and revoke compromised tokens. Run a real two-device join and finish before reopening. Record stranded matches and tell affected testers how to rejoin.
4. **After the session.** Within one day, group reports by build, match ID, reproducibility and player impact. Prioritize failed sessions first, then repeated moments where players could see no answer. Assign owner, next experiment/fix, test and retest date. Bring the next session one clear change and the previous comparison build.
